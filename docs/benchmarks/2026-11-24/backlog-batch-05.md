## What this says about OrbStack

[Batch milestone on the GitHub register](https://github.com/castlemilk/micropod/issues/227#issuecomment-7119218634)

The latest historical four-vCPU holdout measured 2,710 MiB/s on Micropod and 3,010 MiB/s on OrbStack. Micropod won 7 of 15 jointly clean pairs from 16 attempts. Its median paired throughput ratio was 0.854, with a 95% interval of 0.709–1.259. A separate diagnostic in batch four estimated an extra 0.40–0.64 nonrunnable vCPU thread-seconds per wall second for Micropod across three clean pairs. That observation leaves guest idle time, I/O waits and locking as possible contributors. [Historical holdout](../2026-11-22/gamma-tcp-contrasts.md#end-to-end-vm) · [vCPU evidence](backlog-batch-04.md#what-the-scheduling-trace-resolves)

This batch identifies two places where a queue can hide the distinction. A short reply can sit behind already accepted bulk work, either in a sending TCP socket or in the relay’s ready queue. The two fixtures measured different queues. Independently, a guest device can credit transmitted bytes even when redirect delivery fails. Attributing the gap requires correlating destination byte progress with guest and relay readiness in the same scored window. This cycle produced zero new VM or matched-peer attempts.
