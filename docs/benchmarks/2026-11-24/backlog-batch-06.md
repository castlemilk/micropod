# Micropod host-published TCP · Batch 6

24 November 2026 · Ten new comparisons, 60 across six batches. The six batches have tested 33 distinct directions in the existing register of 108. Several directions have multiple controlled comparisons. Earlier studies also contain evidence for directions outside these batches. [Previous batch](backlog-batch-05.md) · [Optimization register](../../research/2026-11-23-optimization-register.md)

**Service-side fair queueing is the strongest throughput lead from this batch.** In the older VM’s stock NIC configuration, adding fair queueing at the service increased median paired delivered throughput by 17.7%. Median fresh p95 fell from 52.7 to 39.1 ms, and the number of attempts at or below 75 ms improved from 3/4 to 4/4. With fair queueing already installed on the NIC, the service change raised both throughput and latency. All four arms completed their 400 fresh requests, and every arm had zero reopens. The original client issued fresh requests sequentially, so bulk/fresh overlap varied. Four pairs in each condition leave substantial uncertainty. ## What remains for a VM verdict

1. **Revised stock baseline.** The 12:49 AEDT read-only Cuttlefish snapshot for project `cuttlefish` showed fresh reports, 0/6 slots leased on Gamma-128GB.local and 1/1 on isolated Linux. The warm-CI checkout was at `ad0646d6292076b6ddf7647f2dc806d785d8f8fb`. The owner’s latest handoff still requested a released window, serving scope and effective disk clearance. No owner-qualified revised stock receipt was available. The first admitted experiment remains four vCPUs, two workers, the stock kernel and direct Reno path. Reuse a qualified receipt if the owner produces it first.
2. **Fixed worker comparison.** Compare two and four workers at four vCPUs, one listener and 128 callback events. The stock dispatcher already spreads the benchmark’s long-lived connections evenly in the host fixture. A live VM comparison will show whether extra workers increase receiver-delivered bytes despite that balance. Listener distribution, same-kernel bridge/direct, read size and four versus six vCPUs each retain their own follow-up.
3. **Locate the limiting stage.** The historical four-vCPU comparison measured 2,710 versus 3,010 MiB/s, 7/15 Micropod wins in common clean pairs from 16 first attempts, with median throughput ratio 0.854 and 95% interval 0.709–1.259. The separate scheduling diagnostic found more nonrunnable vCPU time on Micropod. The actual guest completion queue, active polling, relay readiness and receiver byte progress still need observation in the same interval. The queue models do not assign the historical gap to any one cause. [Peer comparison](../2026-11-22/gamma-tcp-contrasts.md#end-to-end-vm) · [Scheduling diagnostic](backlog-batch-04.md)
4. **Independent OrbStack holdout.** Confirm peer access, bind both already-serving scopes and equal budgets, and collect all 16 randomized, counterbalanced first attempts. Score receiver throughput, fresh losses and reopens, fresh p95/p99 and per-process CPU/GiB and runnable time. The agreed win requires every Micropod attempt to deliver cleanly with fresh p95 ≤75 ms, at least 15 common clean pairs and a lower 95% paired throughput bound above 1.

No timed Gamma work, live VM fixture preflight or peer access probe ran this cycle. Fresh attempts on this host used low-impact fixed workloads. The only VM throughput figures above come from offline verification of an archived series.
