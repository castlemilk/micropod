## What the queue limit actually controlled

With a modeled 512 KiB byte-queue allowance, the compiled Linux 7.3.5 virtio transmit function accepted all 32 offered 63 KiB buffers when its TX NAPI weight was zero. The ring received 2,064,384 bytes and the BQL queued-byte counter stayed at zero. With TX NAPI weight 64, the counter advanced and the stack stopped after nine buffers, 580,608 bytes. The ninth buffer crossed the allowance. The device withheld completions and both arms had spare ring descriptors. These are admissions to the modeled ring. They reveal a specific reason that reading a configured byte limit alone cannot identify where bulk data is waiting. The effective TX mode in the older VM comparisons remains unknown. [Original and candidate observations](batch07-nic/tx_bql.json)

The native relay exposed a different distinction. A 16 KiB unsent-data threshold suppressed most write-ready events, yet every eager socket write still succeeded. Conversely, requesting a 32 KiB writable-event threshold could wake an actual TCP socket with just 8 KiB available. For the next diagnostic, the relevant quantities are bytes the sender admitted, bytes the queue charged, and bytes the receiver delivered in the same interval. None of these experiments measures the revised VM’s throughput gap. [Unsent-data experiment](batch07-tcp/230.10-darwin-write-readiness.receipt.md) · [Pending-write experiment](batch07-relay/write-lowat.receipt.md)
