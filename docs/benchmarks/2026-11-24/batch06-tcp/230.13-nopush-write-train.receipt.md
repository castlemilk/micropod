**24 November 2026 · 230.13 · Native TCP_NOPUSH on a batch of small bulk writes**

Local/host-only Darwin 25.5.0 arm64 on private IPv4 loopback. The stock relay sets TCP_NODELAY in PublishedTCP.c at Micropod `17bcd4355234b11da3a7a1da2ded52f4dc20f5d1`. Both arms used that setting. The only change enabled TCP_NOPUSH during 66 application writes and cleared it at the known batch boundary. Both arms made the same final option call, and the receiver started reading the body after that call. The bursts comprised a 148-byte header, 64 separate 1,024-byte writes and a 148-byte trailer. The kernel reported a 16,332-byte MSS.

Sixteen first-attempt pairs, eight in each order, shuffled before execution. The 61-packet reduction occurred in every pair, with zero sender-reported retransmissions. The bounded work saved packets on this fragmented write pattern. It also left the last 504 bytes unsent until flush. Receiver-readable values are immediate snapshots. Neither those snapshots nor local TCP counters measure wire segmentation, on-wire loss or the time the trailer waited. Fresh p95/p99, elapsed throughput, process CPU/GiB and runnable time were unmeasured.

Keep stock. Before spending a VM trial on coalescing, record the distribution of consecutive small writes on the actual bulk socket and establish a reliable flush boundary. The earlier Linux single-512-KiB-write study and this native 66-write study answer different workload questions.
