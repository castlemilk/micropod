**24 November 2026 · 231.05 · Does an explicit Darwin ECN offer produce a negotiated ECN readback?**

Local/host-only Darwin 25.5.0 arm64, one private IPv4 loopback listener. Both arms used TCP_NODELAY and the listener kept TCP_ENABLE_ECN enabled, read back as 1 on all 32 connections. The stock client inherited its default ECN setting. The sole change explicitly enabled TCP_ENABLE_ECN on the candidate client before connect. This qualifies a native sender-side request and its observation method for a later guest-path trial.

Sixteen randomized order-balanced pairs, eight in each order, all first attempts retained. Before the handshake the client policy read 0 in 16/16 controls and 1 in 16/16 candidates. Immediately after the handshake, the negotiated TCP_CONNECTION_INFO ECN bit was clear on both endpoints in 16/16 controls and 16/16 candidates. Every options mask was 7 (timestamps, SACK and window scaling). Every endpoint received its exact 20-byte request or 16,384-byte response and observed EOF. The two socket retransmission counters summed to zero in each arm. Repetitions share one host and do not estimate between-host variation.

A successful per-socket opt-in did not yield a positive negotiation readback on this loopback path. Neither a SYN trace nor CE marking was part of this fixture. Classic versus Accurate ECN negotiation and packet loss remain undetermined here. Fresh p95/p99, elapsed throughput, CPU/GiB and runnable time were unmeasured. Keep stock. The guest path needs both handshake directions, including AE and TCP options, and a successful negotiation readback before considering any ECN performance comparison.
