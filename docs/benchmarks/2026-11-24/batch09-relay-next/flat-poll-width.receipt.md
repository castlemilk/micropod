### 24 November 2026 · 230.03 · Widening one flat kqueue poll from 24 to 64 events

**Delivered throughput was 4,603.0 → 4,148.0 MiB/s** (arm medians). The wider poll won 1/6 pairs. Median paired change −15.2%, 95% paired bootstrap interval −39.4% to +1.5% (20,000 whole-block resamples).

**Evidence tier and workload.** Archived VM, all original measurement records verified offline. On 21 November, one Gamma four-vCPU Linux 6.18.35 guest served 32 streams and 240 scheduled fresh requests per six-second scored window. Each arm used its own detached process running the same flat relay executable against the same guest service. Only the maximum number of events requested per kevent call changed, from 24 to 64. Both used 256 KiB reads and the same 128-event callback-loop threshold. Six rotating five-arm blocks placed 24 first relative to 64 in four blocks. The earlier source fixture narrowed the poll to eight and did not supply this VM comparison.

**Delivery and latency.** All 12 first attempts passed the archived clean-client checks. Both arms recorded 1,440 fresh arrivals, zero request errors and zero bulk reopens. Every stream advanced, and per-stream byte counts sum to the scored receiver totals. The median round fresh p95/p99 rose from 36.17/59.12 to 60.27/116.89 ms. The control met the 75 ms p95 criterion in 6/6 rounds and the wider poll in 4/6. Wire drops, process CPU/GiB, runnable time and actual fill of individual kevent returns are unavailable in this series.

**Verdict.** Keep the poll width at 24 for the revised worker-count screen. The wider poll missed the fresh-latency criterion twice and provided no supported throughput gain. This detached historical relay cannot establish a rate for the revised stock build.
