### 24 November 2026 · 232.01 · Direct routing for fresh requests while bulk stays on the published port

Archived VM, raw-verified. At a fixed 32-stream, 6 GiB published download, only the route of 240 fresh requests changed: published loopback → direct guest address on the same container. The fresh client started about 40 ms after bulk launch. Relay profiling was on in both arms. All six alternating first-attempt pairs completed, three per order, 36 GiB and 1,440 fresh requests per arm, zero recorded client failures, bulk reopens or incomplete fresh phase samples.

**Delivered throughput:** 4,894.0 → 5,391.4 MiB/s (arm medians), median paired +5.1%, 5/6 wins. Descriptive 95% paired bootstrap interval −3.3% to +21.0% from all 46,656 six-block resamples; fixed alternating order, small sample. Median round fresh p95/p99 improved from 6.32/8.02 → 3.85/4.79 ms.

**Why this matters for selection:** the fresh train lasted 1.140 → 0.473 s (arm medians). Even with perfect alignment, its duration could cover at most 92.0% → 41.4% of the bulk transfer (median of each round's upper bound). Every direct arm's upper bound was below 43%. Thus directing short requests off the published path left much more of the bulk transfer free of concurrent fresh traffic. Whole-transfer rates cannot isolate a sustained-contention throughput gain. Raw phase start timestamps, per-round packet drops, CPU/GiB and runnable time were unavailable for this contrast.

**Verdict and next check:** Faster fresh service in this archived fixture; throughput inconclusive. Keep fresh arrivals on a fixed schedule spanning the entire scored window before selecting a split route. Keep the released stock and worker-count measurements first.
