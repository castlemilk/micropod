### 24 November 2026 · 232.01 · Does connection tracking limit throughput on an already-routed guest endpoint?

**Delivered throughput** was **3,027.3 → 3,302.9 MiB/s** by arm median, tracking enabled → bypassed on the same private routed address. The median *paired* change was **−1.56%** (exploratory 95% percentile bootstrap interval −13.34% to +16.39%, all 46,656 whole-block resamples). Bypass won 3/6 pairs, exactly the three in which it ran first. Each pair’s earlier arm won. The historical report compared each alternative with ordinary NAT. This comparison isolates tracking after routing is held fixed.

**Archived VM evidence, raw first attempts verified locally.** One four-vCPU Gamma worker and one four-CPU service, the same two-worker Mac relay and listener, one 1 GiB service container, Reno gateway route with a 20 ms RTO floor. Only the guest’s decision to track the benchmark flow changed. All 12 pre-window policy readings showed the expected state. A separate eight-request verification observed 48 inbound and 32 outbound packets crossing the two bypass rules. Scored workload was 32 bulk streams and 240 scheduled fresh requests per six-second window. Analysis used all six original blocks in their recorded rotating order.

**Guardrails.** Each arm delivered bytes on all original streams in 6/6 attempts, with 1,440 scheduled fresh arrivals, zero reported request errors and zero reopens. Median per-attempt fresh p95/p99 was **101.74/148.03 → 114.87/246.37 ms**. Only 1/6 tracking and 2/6 bypass attempts met 75 ms p95. Scored-window packet drops, per-process CPU/GiB and runnable time were unavailable.

**Verdict.** Keep connection tracking. The larger standalone throughput median disappears in the paired comparison and the median fresh tails worsened. The next live check remains the revised four-vCPU/two-worker stock baseline. A future tracking trial would need sustained steady state and independently randomized ordering.
