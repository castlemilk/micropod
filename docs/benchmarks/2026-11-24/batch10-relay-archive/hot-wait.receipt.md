### 24 November 2026 · 230.03 · Wait up to 50 µs for more bulk events on a host relay callback

**Archived VM path, locally raw-verified.** On 21 November a standalone Darwin relay on Gamma's M5 Max forwarded a loopback listener to the same four-vCPU Micropod Linux service. The two executables used the same relay source, direct endpoint and serial dispatch wrapper. After a read of at least 64 KiB, both allowed up to four further kqueue drains during a 200 µs loop budget. Only the per-drain wait changed, from zero to 50 µs. This was an experimental relay on an older guest path.

Per arm, the six single-stream runs delivered all 4.5 GiB with zero reopens. The burst client completed 18 synchronized bursts, each with 32 verified-length 8 MiB responses, for another 4.5 GiB per arm. All 600 fresh marker requests per arm completed before the burst phases. Each block contributes one median of its three burst rates. The intervals resample whole paired blocks, with all 46,656 ordered six-block resamples. Six blocks from scheduled orders leave substantial uncertainty. A sustained mixed-load p95/p99, packet drops, per-process CPU/GiB and runnable time are unavailable for this contrast.

**Verdict.** Keep the bounded wait as a 32-stream burst candidate. Single-stream throughput was inconclusive. Before adopting it, test the revised relay with continuous mixed arrivals and measure CPU cost and loaded tails. Its 50 µs wait may occupy a worker needed by a new connection during that interval. The current stock baseline and fixed worker comparison retain priority.
