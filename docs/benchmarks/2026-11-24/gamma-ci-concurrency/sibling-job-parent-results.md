# CPU cgroup parents for 32 CI jobs on Gamma

Keep the flat cgroup layout for the measured CI workload. Giving each job and service a common CPU parent increased Micropod’s scheduled HTTP p95 by 157.10%, p99 by 74.96%, and the eight-build makespan by 1.54%, using median within-seed, within-round ratios. The peer kept its policy throughout. Its corresponding p95 ratio was 0.933. The candidate keeps the same signed Mac executable, Linux bootstrap, guest kernel and container limits as its flat control. Both ran the same guest-runner binary. [Matched crossover](sibling-job-parent-results.json) · [Frozen product](sibling-job-parent-product.json)

## Workload and actual CPU hierarchy

Each engine had four vCPUs and 4 GiB. Each of 32 CI jobs had an internal network, a service and a client. Eight client containers ran two-revision Go builds with vet, tests, linking and execution of the resulting binaries. The builder and service stayed on the job’s assigned vCPU. All four guest CPUs had two build jobs each. The 32 warmed clients offered a total of 64,000 requests/s over 1.5 seconds. The native 16 KiB Linux guest used the 250 µs ordinary scheduler slice, high-resolution scheduling ticks, the next-buddy wakeup preference, and private 128 MiB compiler scratch. OrbStack retained its own settings. All six native boots used the Virtualization.framework worker. [Experiment](sibling_job_parent.py) · [Previous packaged settings](sibling-ci-dispatch-and-scratch-results.md#fresh-holdout-of-the-final-worker)

In flat mode all 64 workload leaves were siblings under /k8s.io. In the candidate, the runner placed the two payloads of each job under their own taskless parent. The guest-root observer matched each real container PID, its start time, namespace, cgroup inode and singleton CPU mask. It also checked held HTTP clients and real Go/compiler processes outside the timed intervals. The single-use sandbox lifecycle stayed the same. All leaves retained their one-CPU quota. Each new parent had weight 100, no local CPU quota and two workload leaves. The four-CPU root cap was verified. The CRI log independently matched every leaf basename to its create, start, runtime exit PID and sandbox retirement. [Cgroup evidence](sibling_job_parent_evidence.py) · [CRI replay](sibling_job_parent_cri.py) · [Linux cgroup CPU controller](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html#cpu)

The four discovery boots completed 3,072,000 offered requests and 256 two-revision jobs, with 4,096 workload tests. The two held-out boots completed another 1,536,000 requests and 128 jobs with 2,048 tests. Including responses that drained after the last scheduled offer, the smallest margin between the final response and the earliest build exit across all six boots was 1.138 s. ## Checking the mechanism

Two host snapshots about 800 ms apart fell inside every client stream and the lifetime of every builder. Guest clock checks on each side bounded the host/guest time offset. The closest snapshot was 269 ms inside the common interval. After timing, an arm64 loop pinned to each guest CPU added a known number of instructions. All 32 engine/boot/CPU calibrations landed within 97.6–102.8% of the expected increase on the numbered host vCPU. In both native modes, the vCPU that eventually finished last received essentially the same CPU time as the median of the other three during this saturated interval. Its median instruction count was 0.37% higher in flat mode and 1.95% higher with job parents. The counter includes all work on that vCPU. [Raw host counter brackets](sibling-job-parent-midwindow.json) · [Counter replay](sibling_job_parent_host.py) · [Apple XNU resource accounting](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/doc/observability/recount.md)

Across the two grouped boots, none of the new job parents or measured payloads recorded a quota throttle. The worker root recorded small amounts of throttling. Those snapshots enclose suite setup, workload, drain and post-work calibration. Each level is reported independently because parents account for descendant activity. The group intervention changes how CPU time is shared under contention. These measurements do not identify an individual kernel operation responsible for the latency increase. ## Failed observation and cleanup

The first flat attempt finished its four dense rounds. During the final hierarchy scan, the temporary trigger container exited and its cgroup disappeared. Its CRI identity was separate from the 64 measured workloads. That attempt is excluded from every result above. On retry, the worker waited for the trigger to retire and still required every requested inode and live resident. Each qualified worker confirmed its 64 flat paths or 96 grouped paths were gone before shutdown and unmounted its observer. Six in-memory changes to real receipts were rejected, including a surviving parent, a doubled root quota, changed offered demand, a missing response, switched job membership and a foreign guest clock.
