# Gamma CI worker storage on dedicated APFS drives

Keep host caching enabled on the worker’s writable virtio disk for this workload. On four dedicated APFS volumes, bypassing the cache increased scheduled HTTP p95 by a paired 4.69% and post-dispatch p95 by 7.05%. The eight-build batch changed by −0.42%. Its direction reversed between the two seeds. Micropod beat OrbStack’s scheduled p95 and p99 in all 16 paired windows, and OrbStack finished 12 of the 16 build batches first. ## The actual CI workload and disk

Each engine ran 64 containers on four vCPUs and 4 GiB, with an internal network for each of 32 service/client pairs. Eight clients built, vetted, tested, linked and ran two Go revisions each. The 32 warmed clients offered 64,000 HTTP requests/s over 1.5 seconds. All 3,072,000 requests completed across the dedicated-volume series, alongside 256 two-revision builds and 4,096 test passes. Even the last HTTP response finished more than 1.168 s before the earliest build exit. [Workload replay](sibling_worker_disk_cache_results.py)

Both settings used the same signed Mac executable and Linux guest binaries. The running worker reported the URL, VM identity, owner PID, writability, cache mode and synchronization mode of its actual Virtualization.framework disk attachment. The 6 GiB configured worker used a 6,576,668,672-byte raw ext4 image on each 16 GiB sparse APFS volume. Both cache modes used Apple’s best-effort `fsync` synchronization. [Signed product](sibling-worker-disk-cache-product.json) · [Apple synchronization semantics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/virtualization/vzdiskimagesynchronizationmode/fsync.md)

After timing, every native boot successfully wrote and synced a private 1 MiB payload and checked its SHA-256 on three reads. The guest cold and direct reads each fetched 1 MiB from the virtio block device. The warm reread fetched zero device bytes. The guest recorded two separate flushes per calibration. Across the same calibration brackets, macOS attributed zero disk-read bytes to both cached VMMs and 2,220,032 disk-read bytes to each uncached VMM. These are whole-process counters. The measurement also covered the separate process that opened the attachment. ## Spotlight and FSEvents

The provisioning helper creates a fresh APFS sparse image in the owned worker scope, places empty `.fseventsd/no_log` and `.metadata_never_index` files at its volume root, applies `mdutil -i off` to that specific volume, and ejects and remounts it before starting Micropod. Each of four workers had a distinct volume UUID. On every boot, the raw disk’s filesystem device matched the APFS mount and differed from its parent. A fresh `mdutil -s -v` reported indexing and searching disabled at worker readiness and after the workload. All four journals contained zero files and zero bytes at both checkpoints. Each image was ejected and removed. Apple documents `no_log` as the per-volume switch for persistent event history. [First live worker](20261125T050424Z-sibling-worker-disk-azaqu2f8/a/host-volume-live.json) · [Its post-work readback](20261125T050424Z-sibling-worker-disk-azaqu2f8/a/host-volume-before-detach.json) · [Apple FSEvents documentation](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Darwin/Conceptual/FSEvents_ProgGuide/FileSystemEventSecurity/FileSystemEventSecurity.html)

A separate positive control performed the same 24 file writes and renames on two newly created volumes. After a clean remount, the control had Spotlight enabled and two persistent journal files totaling 542 bytes. The excluded volume reported Spotlight disabled and zero journal bytes. Both images and their directories were removed. A preliminary mount using `-nobrowse` returned an unknown Spotlight state. It was excluded before the CI worker trials. [Paired volume calibration](worker-apfs-drive-calibration.json)

The first worker launch on the ordinary filesystem exposed different macOS spellings for the same temporary directory. The signed selector missed its marker, the verifier rejected the boot before timing, and cleanup succeeded. The revised selector checks the resolved disk, workspace and worker identities before enabling the experiment. Fourteen in-memory substitutions against a real dedicated-volume archive were rejected, including enabled Spotlight, a wrong raw disk device, a missing FSEvents marker, an unremoved attachment and an altered warm-read checksum. ## After a clean eject

A fifth dedicated volume ran the build-phase diagnostic with another 768,000 completed requests, 64 two-revision builds and 1,024 test passes. After the worker stopped and the writable image was ejected, the fixture remounted that same image read-only. macOS still reported indexing and searching disabled. Both markers were intact, and the persisted FSEvents journal had **zero files and zero bytes**. The volume, container and partition UUIDs matched, as did the inode and size of `worker.ext4`. The helper ejected the read-only mount and removed its backing file. Four further substitutions were rejected, including a writable remount and a changed post-eject disk inode. New storage and build-phase runs use the dedicated-volume provisioner through their regular entry points. [Read-only remount and final eject](20261125T051112Z-sibling-build-phases-5fsnq3a6/a/host-volume-detached.json) · [Volume and complete workload replay](sibling-build-phases-volume.json) · [Provisioning and retirement](worker_apfs_drive.py)

In this instrumented boot, OrbStack finished three of four build batches first. For the two slots that finished last on Micropod in each round, the first revision’s test command took 26.3 ms longer than the same slots on OrbStack at the paired median. Its vet step was 31.4 ms faster. Across all slots, the first revision’s native test command was 2.2 ms faster at the paired median. The median marker round trip was 56 µs on Micropod and 41 µs on OrbStack. Micropod’s leaf I/O pressure increased by at most 0.369 ms in any one measured phase. The peer did not expose leaf pressure counters. The first test overlapped the offered HTTP traffic, and every second test started after the traffic drained. The eight selected native jobs recorded zero I/O pressure increments and zero CPU quota throttling during their test steps. Two of those eight still had a faster first test than the matched OrbStack job. The counters include the HTTP client sharing the builder’s cgroup.
